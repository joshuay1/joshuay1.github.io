## Framing

One branching history diagram connects inherited institutions and reforms, followed by everyday and civic examples, collective intelligence and parallel research fields. A short Kultur Komitee introduction explains the setting and Joshua’s role before the first step. Projects illustrate each step, with a matching number and label on every example. The closing asks students to explore a project or process, research its context and consider different possibilities before deciding what to build.

Collective intelligence asks how groups combine knowledge and capabilities. Democratic theory also concerns inclusion, legitimate authority and the right to contest a result. See [Landemore’s chapter on inclusive deliberation](https://doi.org/10.23943/princeton/9780691155654.003.0004).

The analogy is that people can inspect, borrow and revise institutional rules. This is not a complete family tree, a literal software-development process or an inevitable progression toward better democracy. Arrows represent selected documented influences. Political rights and the power to change rules have historically been unequal; reforms involve interests, legitimacy and tradeoffs.

- The 1787 constitutional compromise combined a House apportioned by population with two senators per state. [US Senate](https://www.senate.gov/about/origins-foundations/senate-and-constitution.htm)
- The 1848 Swiss Constitution established a bicameral federal parliament, using the United States as a model. [Swiss Parliament: 1848](https://www.parlament.ch/de/%C3%BCber-das-parlament/Seiten/Ablauf-Wahlen-1848.aspx)
- Australia combines parliamentary government responsible to the elected house, drawn from Westminster, with a federal Senate inspired by the United States. [Australian Parliament](https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/your-questions-on-notice/questions/what-is-the-washminster-system)
- The Australian Constitution took effect with federation on 1 January 1901. [Australian Constitution](https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/how-parliament-works/the-australian-constitution/the-australian-constitution-in-focus)
- The Parliament Acts of 1911 and 1949 permit certain public bills to pass without Lords consent. The 1949 Act reduced the delay for eligible non-money bills from two years to one. Money bills and other categories have different provisions; the Lords did not lose every veto. [UK Parliament](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/sn00675/)
- The optional federal legislative referendum dates to 1874. The mandatory constitutional referendum already existed in 1848. [Swiss Parliament: referendums](https://www.parlament.ch/de/%C3%BCber-das-parlament/Seiten/faktenblatt-referenden.aspx)
- The federal popular initiative for partial constitutional revision dates to 1891. The initiative for total revision predates this, going back to 1848. [Swiss Parliament: initiatives](https://www.parlament.ch/de/%C3%BCber-das-parlament/Seiten/faktenblatt-volksinitiativen.aspx)
- Preferential voting for the House of Representatives was introduced in 1918; compulsory voting at federal elections in 1924. Senate proportional representation was enacted in 1948 and first used in 1949, not at federation in 1901. [Australian Electoral Commission](https://www.aec.gov.au/Elections/history-of-electoral-reform.htm)
- Political participation was historically restricted. Swiss women gained federal voting rights in 1971. [Swiss Federal Archives](https://www.bar.admin.ch/en/the-swiss-state-and-its-citizens-after-1848)

## Existing processes and prototype ideas

### Swiss direct democracy

**Process:** A federal popular initiative proposes a constitutional change; an optional referendum challenges legislation.

**A concrete difficulty:** A voter or organizer needs to understand the text and the published procedural status.

**Possible prototype:** A comparison of the proposal with the status quo, or a tracker using official records.

**Evaluation:** Can a reader find the relevant passage and explain what is being proposed?

Sources: [Popular initiatives](https://www.ch.ch/en/votes-and-elections/popular-initiative/), [Referendums](https://www.ch.ch/en/votes-and-elections/referendum/).

### ETH student representation

**Process:** Fachvereine and VSETH represent students in departmental and ETH-wide bodies; VSETH’s members’ council governs its own budget, elections and statutes.

**A concrete difficulty:** A student needs to identify who can act and what has already been discussed.

**Possible prototype:** An agenda brief or issue record connecting a concern, evidence, the responsible body and its response.

**Evaluation:** Can another student identify the responsible body and the present status?

Sources: [VSETH politics](https://vseth.ethz.ch/politik/), [VSETH structure](https://vseth.ethz.ch/struktur/).

### Citizens’ assemblies and consultations

**Process:** Participants examine evidence, discuss alternatives and produce recommendations or consultation outputs.

**A concrete difficulty:** An important concern can disappear when discussions are summarized.

**Possible prototype:** A source-linked correction or unresolved-question record.

**Evaluation:** Compare the account with original contributions; count unsupported claims and missing requirements.

Sources: [Irish citizens’ assemblies](https://citizensassembly.ie/about/faq/), [vTaiwan](https://info.vtaiwan.tw/), [Murmi project](https://joshuacyang.com/projects/murmi/).

### Participatory budgeting

**Process:** Develop proposals, assess feasibility and allocate a limited budget using a defined procedure.

**A concrete difficulty:** A proposer needs to meet requirements; a participant needs to understand how the allocation was produced.

**Possible prototype:** A feasibility checklist, comparison of rules, or readable allocation receipt.

**Evaluation:** Hold ballots and costs fixed for a rule comparison; ask a participant to explain an allocation.

Sources: [Decide Madrid process](https://decide.madrid.es/mas-informacion/dm/presupuestos), [Komitee Equal Shares](https://joshuacyang.com/pubs/komitee-equal-shares/), [Public KK26 receipts](https://equalshares.kulturkomitee.win/).

### Accountability after a decision

**Process:** Connect an adopted proposal to responsibility, implementation and public reporting.

**A concrete difficulty:** A resident can see that a proposal was approved but cannot tell what happened next.

**Possible prototype:** A proposal-to-implementation record using existing milestones and evidence.

**Evaluation:** Can a reader identify the current status, responsible body and supporting record?

Sources: [Decidim accountability component](https://docs.decidim.org/en/develop/admin/components/accountability.html).

## Parallel research directions

This is a selected reading guide, not a chronology or exhaustive map. Recent topics include AI-mediated deliberation, allocation under feasibility constraints, and grounded or inspectable agents.

### Computational social choice

Voting rules, proportional allocation and fair selection of representatives.

- [Peters, Pierczyński & Skowron (2021). Proportional Participatory Budgeting with Additive Utilities](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/69f8ea31de0c00502b2ae571fbab1f95-Abstract.html). _NeurIPS_. **Peer-reviewed mathematical paper.** Develops proportionality axioms and allocation rules for projects with different costs and additive utilities; a foundation for the Method of Equal Shares. **Limit:** A guarantee depends on its preference model and precise axiom. It does not make every outcome fair under every definition.

- [Flanigan, Gölz, Gupta, Hennig & Procaccia (2021). Fair algorithms for selecting citizens’ assemblies](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03788-6). _Nature_. **Peer-reviewed algorithms and deployment study.** Optimizes selection probabilities among volunteers while meeting demographic quotas; evaluates fairness on data from ten assemblies. **Limit:** Fairness within the volunteer pool does not solve unequal willingness or ability to volunteer.

- [Skowron (2026). A General Theory of Proportionality with Additive Utilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08504v1). _arXiv:2602.08504_. **Preprint · February 2026.** Proposes proportional rules for scored preferences under general feasibility constraints, and proportional rankings. **Limit:** A theoretical proposal, not a deployment evaluation. Constraints and the definition of proportionality must be specified.

### Deliberative democracy

Inclusion, reason-giving, public judgment and legitimate authority.

- [Woolley et al. (2010). Evidence for a Collective Intelligence Factor in the Performance of Human Groups](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1193147). _Science_. **Peer-reviewed empirical study.** Two studies with 699 people found a shared factor in small groups’ performance across tasks, associated with social sensitivity and conversational turn-taking. **Limit:** These associations do not show that equal speaking time causes better decisions, or measure democratic legitimacy.

- [Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer & Helbing (2011). How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3107299/). _PNAS_. **Peer-reviewed laboratory experiment.** In numerical estimation tasks with 144 participants, seeing others’ estimates reduced diversity and increased confidence without a corresponding improvement in collective accuracy. **Limit:** An estimation experiment is not a general verdict against discussion; task and interaction structure matter.

### Human–AI interaction

How people use, question and correct AI assistance during participation.

- [Tessler et al. (2024). AI can help humans find common ground in democratic deliberation](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq2852). _Science_. **Peer-reviewed experiments.** The Habermas Machine uses opinions and critiques to revise group statements. Across experiments with 5,734 participants, AI statements were preferred to those from human mediators. **Limit:** Preference for a statement is not proof of factual accuracy, legitimate authority, or lasting political agreement.

- [Yang, Hausladen, Peters, Pournaras, Hänggli Fricker & Helbing (2024). Designing Digital Voting Systems for Citizens: Achieving Fairness and Legitimacy in Participatory Budgeting](https://doi.org/10.1145/3665332). _Digital Government: Research and Practice_. **Peer-reviewed empirical article.** Studies how voting-system design shapes citizens’ perceptions of fairness and legitimacy in participatory budgeting. **Limit:** Perceived fairness and actual understanding are different outcomes; evaluate them separately.



### Complexity science & collective intelligence

- [Woolley et al. (2010). Evidence for a Collective Intelligence Factor in the Performance of Human Groups](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1193147). _Science_. **Peer-reviewed empirical study.** Two studies with 699 people found a shared factor in small groups’ performance across tasks, associated with social sensitivity and conversational turn-taking. **Limit:** These associations do not show that equal speaking time causes better decisions, or measure democratic legitimacy.

- [Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer & Helbing (2011). How social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3107299/). _PNAS_. **Peer-reviewed laboratory experiment.** In numerical estimation tasks with 144 participants, seeing others’ estimates reduced diversity and increased confidence without a corresponding improvement in collective accuracy. **Limit:** An estimation experiment is not a general verdict against discussion; task and interaction structure matter.

The following agent studies are optional further reading; the talk does not include a Belief Engine demonstration.

How collective learning and judgment emerge from interactions, networks and feedback. This differs from computational social choice’s study of formal decision rules. Computational social science provides methods that can serve several of these research directions.

- [Park et al. (2026 revision). LLM Agents Grounded in Self-Reports Enable General-Purpose Simulation of Individuals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v3). _arXiv:2411.10109v3_. **Preprint · June 2026 revision of a 2024 paper.** Builds agents from interviews and/or surveys of 1,052 Americans and evaluates predictions on held-out responses against people’s own test–retest consistency. **Limit:** Predictive fidelity on studied tasks does not confer consent or political authority. The earlier title was Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People.

- [Yang, Flechtner, Dailisan & Bakker (2026). Belief Engine: Configurable and Inspectable Stance Dynamics in Multi-Agent LLM Deliberation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15343). _arXiv:2605.15343_. **Preprint · May 2026.** Maintains explicit proposition-level stances with configurable evidence uptake and prior anchoring, recording the evidence associated with updates. **Limit:** Evidence scoring and update rules are modeling choices. An inspectable trace is not by itself a faithful simulation of a citizen.

## Evidence and evaluation

The rule comparison uses nine invented ballots. The Polis schematic uses invented groups and is not a clustering analysis of vTaiwan. The summary exercise and agent trace are illustrations. Swiss AI Futures photographs are real public project media. The Kultur Komitee examples use user-provided fieldwork photographs. The 2025 Komitee deployment figures and 2026 KK26 receipt are separate evidence.

A few usability sessions can reveal difficulties. They do not establish a population effect. A theorem gives a guarantee under assumptions; an experiment compares outcomes under a study design; a deployment documents a setting. Keep those claims distinct.

For a hackathon, identify a person’s task, the existing workflow and a specific difficulty. Build one support tool and compare it with what that person uses today. An uncomplicated idea can be useful when it fits the task.

## Additional readings connected to the four directions

Representation, consent, power and responsibility in civic systems.

- [Selbst, boyd, Friedler, Venkatasubramanian & Vertesi (2019). Fairness and Abstraction in Sociotechnical Systems](https://doi.org/10.1145/3287560.3287598). \*FAT\*\*. **Peer-reviewed conceptual paper.** Explains how isolating a technical model from its social setting can undermine fairness goals. **Limit:** A conceptual analysis of system design, not an empirical guarantee that a particular civic tool is fair.

- [Bisbee, Clinton, Dorff, Kenkel & Larson (2024). Synthetic Replacements for Human Survey Data? The Perils of Large Language Models](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2024.5). _Political Analysis_. **Peer-reviewed empirical audit.** Synthetic responses could match survey averages while understating variation and changing statistical relationships. Results were sensitive to prompts and collection dates. **Limit:** The study tests particular models and tasks; use its evaluation questions rather than assuming identical failures for every later model.

How digital tools fit institutions, workflows and public accountability.

- [Small, Bjorkegren, Erkkilä, Shaw & Megill (2021). Polis: Scaling Deliberation by Mapping High Dimensional Opinion Spaces](https://www.e-revistes.uji.es/index.php/recerca/article/view/5516). _Recerca_. **Peer-reviewed system and case study.** Describes a system that maps agreement patterns and surfaces statements supported across opinion groups, with real consultation cases. **Limit:** A useful map of participants is not a representative estimate of an entire population or evidence that consensus is always desirable.

- [Yang, Scheurer & Helbing (2026). Komitee Equal Shares: A Hybrid Algorithm for Priceable Participatory Funding](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3798958). _CHI Extended Abstracts_. **Published conference extended abstract · field deployment.** Combines personal preferences with group evaluations and allocation receipts. In Winterthur in 2025, 38 citizens allocated CHF 380,000 to 43 projects. **Limit:** One deployment, with 32 survey respondents. Simulated comparisons do not isolate every component’s causal contribution.
