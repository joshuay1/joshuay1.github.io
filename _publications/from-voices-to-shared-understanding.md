### From Conversation to a Shared Record

In citizen deliberation, people need a say in how their contributions are understood as well as an opportunity to speak. A voting card or summary can preserve the subject of a contribution while dropping its conditions, uncertainty or disagreement.

We developed [Murmi]({{ '/projects/murmi/' | relative_url }}) to explore **dynamic participatory sensemaking**: an ongoing relationship between live discussion, voting on summarised position cards, and reviewing the summaries that bring those views together. AI helps prepare the material; moderators and participants can question what it says and return to the conversation.

### The Swiss AI Futures Workshops

The study covers four language-group workshops at two Swiss events, in Zurich and Lausanne, using German, English and French. Participants first experienced a human-moderated workflow and then an AI-supported workflow. The paper combines questionnaire responses from **76 participants** with written comments and records of cards, votes and summary review.

Participants valued opportunities to express their views and challenge wording, emphasis and missing concerns. They also described a tension: additional ways to contribute through voting and typing did not always provide the exchange and listening they valued in face-to-face discussion.

### What the Findings Support

The findings point to practical requirements for participatory summaries: time to inspect them together, ways to challenge how contributions are represented, and follow-through on requests for revision. Approving a proposition or accepting a summary does not by itself establish consensus or authorise publication on the group's behalf.

### Scope and Limitations

The workflows were used in a fixed order, and topics, languages, group sizes and facilitation varied. The findings describe the workshop arrangements as a whole and cannot isolate the effect of AI. The records also did not consistently establish whether participants felt their concerns were resolved.
